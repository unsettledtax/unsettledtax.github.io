The karta’s unlimited liability may decide your HUF’s US tax classification

No IRS ruling has ever classified a Hindu Undivided Family. The one detailed practitioner analysis that exists gets the trust-versus-business-entity question right and stops there — without reaching the Treasury regulation that decides which business entity it would be if it isn’t a trust.

Sources read in full · 26 CFR 301.7701-4(a) · 26 CFR 301.7701-3(b)(2) · 26 CFR 301.7701-2(b)(8) · Hindu law on karta and coparcener liability

If a coparcener of an HUF becomes a US citizen or green-card holder, every downstream US filing obligation — Form 3520, Form 8938, Form 8865, Form 5471, FBAR, none of them, all of them — turns on one question the IRS has never answered: what kind of entity is a Hindu Undivided Family under 26 U.S.C. §7701? This page does not resolve that question. It reads the two regulations that would have to be applied in sequence to answer it, and finds a second, narrower question inside the first one that no US-tax source appears to have asked at all.

The one most often missed

1. If HUF is a business entity, the karta’s liability — not the coparceners’ — decides whether it defaults to a partnership or a corporation

The best existing analysis (a 2020 article from a US international tax firm, read in full) correctly frames the threshold question as trust versus business entity under 26 CFR 301.7701-4(a):

26 CFR 301.7701-4(a): “An arrangement will be treated as a trust under the Internal Revenue Code if it can be shown that the purpose of the arrangement is to vest in trustees responsibility for the protection and conservation of property for beneficiaries who cannot share in the discharge of this responsibility and, therefore, are not associates in a joint enterprise for the conduct of business for profit.”

That article concludes — reasonably — that a non-trading HUF holding family property looks like a trust, and a trading HUF running a family business looks like a business entity, with the classification turning on facts and circumstances. It then briefly lists the consequences of each possible business-entity outcome (Form 5471 if a corporation, Form 8865 if a partnership) without saying which one a business-entity HUF would actually default to. That default is not a coin flip. It is fixed by a specific regulatory test, and Hindu law answers that test cleanly.

26 CFR 301.7701-3(b)(2)(i): unless the entity elects otherwise, a foreign eligible entity is “(A) A partnership if it has two or more members and at least one member does not have limited liability; [or] (B) An association if all members have limited liability.”

26 CFR 301.7701-3(b)(2)(ii), definition of limited liability: “a member of a foreign eligible entity has limited liability if the member has no personal liability for the debts of or claims against the entity by reason of being a member.”

Under Hindu law, the karta and the ordinary coparceners do not have the same liability. A karta who manages a trading HUF’s business can be personally sued and held liable for debts contracted in the conduct of that business — liability that is not capped at the karta’s share of the joint family property. A coparcener who takes no active role, by contrast, is liable only to the extent of their own undivided interest in the family property. A January 2026 Bombay High Court order on execution of a decree against an HUF states the rule directly: the karta has personal liability “only when the HUF is a ‘trading’ HUF which carries on business” — but where that condition is met, the karta’s own assets are exposed, not merely the family property.

Run that fact pattern through 301.7701-3(b)(2)(ii): a trading HUF with a karta who has no cap on personal liability is an entity with “at least one member [who] does not have limited liability.” Under (b)(2)(i)(A), that is the fact pattern for the default partnership classification, not the default association (corporation) classification — regardless of how limited the ordinary coparceners’ exposure is. No source found states this. The best existing article lists Form 8865 (partnership) and Form 5471 (corporation) as parallel possibilities without noting that the regulation’s own default mechanically favours the partnership outcome for exactly the kind of HUF — a trading one, with an active karta — that is most likely to be treated as a business entity at all in the first place.

2. HUF is not on the per se corporation list — so the default question is live

26 CFR 301.7701-2(b)(8)(i) lists, by country, the foreign entity types that are always treated as corporations regardless of the entity’s own facts (an Australian “Public Limited Company,” an Argentine “Sociedad Anonima,” and so on). For India, the list names exactly one entity type:

26 CFR 301.7701-2(b)(8)(i): “India, Public Limited Company.”

An HUF is not a Public Limited Company and is not defined under India’s Companies Act at all — it exists under Hindu personal law, a distinction the 2020 practitioner article also makes correctly. That means an HUF classified as a business entity is an “eligible entity” for check-the-box purposes, which is exactly why the default rule in 301.7701-3(b)(2), and the liability test inside it, is the provision that actually controls — not a per se rule that would make the question moot.

The prior question, still open

All of this is downstream of the trust-versus-business-entity question, which is itself unresolved and fact-dependent, exactly as the 2020 article states. A purely property-holding HUF with a passive karta plausibly stays a trust under 301.7701-4(a), in which case none of the partnership-versus-corporation analysis above applies at all — Form 3520/3520-A and the throwback-tax rules on accumulated trust income become the live questions instead, not Form 8865 or Form 5471. This page does not resolve which HUFs are trades and which are trusts. It resolves a narrower, conditional question: if a particular HUF is a trading, business-entity HUF, which default classification the regulation assigns to it, and why the karta’s exposure (not the coparceners’) is the fact that decides it.

3. What each classification would mean for a coparcener who is a US person

If HUF is…Governing testFilings for a US-person coparcener
A trust301.7701-4(a): protecting/conserving property, not a joint profit enterpriseForm 3520, Form 3520-A (if a US person is treated as an owner or receives a distribution), Form 8938, FBAR; possible throwback tax on accumulated income distributions
A business entity, defaulting to partnership301.7701-3(b)(2)(i)(A): 2+ members, at least one (the karta, in a trading HUF) without limited liabilityForm 8865, Schedule K-1 income reporting, Form 8938
A business entity, defaulting to association/corporation301.7701-3(b)(2)(i)(B): all members have limited liability — the fact pattern this page argues a trading HUF with an active karta does not satisfyForm 5471, CFC/GILTI analysis if control thresholds are met, Form 8938, PFIC exposure if not a CFC

The three columns are not interchangeable inconveniences — the compliance burden, the penalty exposure, and the character of income reported to the IRS differ substantially between them. A coparcener working from the wrong classification is not making a rounding error.

4. Falsification: what was checked before this was written

Search on the market’s actual vocabulary (“HUF US tax classification,” “HUF Form 8865 8938 trust partnership IRS”) surfaces a genuinely competent, detailed article (sherayzenlaw.com, read in full, not title-matched) that quotes 301.7701-4(a) verbatim and correctly frames the trust/business-entity threshold question. A second search combining the Treasury regulation number with Hindu-law liability terms (“HUF karta limited liability 301.7701-3,” “check-the-box default classification”) returned no matching content connecting the two at all — the closest hits were unrelated PDF/Facebook fragments. A separate search on the Hindu-law side (“karta personal liability HUF debts,” “coparcener liability limited to share”) returned multiple convergent legal sources (Legal 500, Legal Bites, a January 2026 Bombay High Court order, and several Indian tax/finance sites) all stating the same unlimited-karta/limited-coparcener asymmetry independently of any US tax context. The gap is not that HUF is uncovered — it is that the Hindu-law liability asymmetry and the Treasury regulation that turns on exactly that asymmetry have not been read together anywhere found.

Questions worth putting to a preparer

  1. Are you treating this HUF as a trust or a business entity, and on what facts — is it primarily a property-holding arrangement, or does it carry on a trade or business through the karta?
  2. If you are treating it as a business entity, have you tested the karta’s actual liability exposure under the personal law governing this specific HUF, rather than assuming a default?
  3. If the karta’s liability is unlimited for the HUF’s business debts, why would the default classification be anything other than a partnership under 301.7701-3(b)(2)(i)(A)?
  4. Has Form 8832 been considered at all, or is the position resting entirely on the default rule?
  5. If treated as a trust, has the throwback-tax exposure on accumulated distributions been modelled, separately from the annual Form 3520/3520-A filing question?
  6. Is this position supported by an IRS ruling on HUF specifically, or is it a reasoned application of a general entity-classification regulation to a fact pattern the IRS has never addressed?
