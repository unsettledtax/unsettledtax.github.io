India totalised your pension service with Germany. Then you moved to America, which has no such deal. EPFO's own text says that doesn't matter — but nobody has said so.

India has Social Security (Totalization) Agreements with 20 countries. The United States is not one of them. Advisors have covered what that means for someone who works only in India, or only in one SSA country. Nobody found addresses the specific, increasingly common case: an Indian professional who worked in India, then a Totalization-Agreement country, then settled in the US — without ever completing ten years of pensionable service anywhere. EPFO's own scheme text answers the question in the worker's favor, on a mechanism no source found states.

Sources read in full · EPFO's EPS Scheme, 1952, para 43-A (verbatim, at epfindia.gov.in) · EPFO Circular IWU/7(17)2009/2816, 25 May 2012 · EPFO's International Workers – Social Security Agreements page · two competitor articles read in full

This is not the ordinary "NRI leaves India, what happens to the PF account" question this family's own Part 8 and general NRI-advisory content already cover well. It is narrower: what happens to the specific Totalization-Agreement machinery — the rule that lets India count time worked in an SSA-partner country toward the ten years of Indian pensionable service needed for a monthly pension — when the worker's next stop after the SSA country is the United States, which has never signed such an agreement with India, despite "years of negotiation" reported as far back as 2021.

None of this contradicts the para 14(1) "date of exit" reading below — that specific mechanism is not what the litigation is about — but see Part 13 for the full picture before treating any citation to para 43-A as settled, current text.

The mechanism, read from EPFO's own scheme text

1. Totalisation and the withdrawal benefit are governed by two short paragraphs, both read here in full

Para 43-A of the Employees' Pension Scheme, 1995 — "Special provisions in respect of International Workers" — substitutes two paragraphs of the main scheme for IWs. Para 9, verbatim:

"in the case of the member covered by a social security agreement, the period of coverage under relevant social security programme in another country shall be added to actual service and the aggregate thereof shall be treated as eligible service as may be provided in the social security [agreement]"

And para 14(1), verbatim, which is the operative withdrawal-benefit clause:

"An International Worker covered under a social security agreement entered into between India and another country who has not rendered the eligible service prescribed in paragraph 9 on the date of exit, or on attaining the age of 58 years, whichever is earlier, shall be entitled to a totalisation benefit as may be provided in the said social security agreement: Provided that if the International worker covered under the provisions of the said agreement has not rendered the eligible service even after including the totalisation benefit as may be provided in the said agreement, then such International Worker shall be entitled to a withdrawal benefit as laid down under Table 'D'."

Two things follow directly from this text. First, the test is anchored to "the date of exit" — the date the worker leaves EPF/EPS-covered employment, not any later date. Second, whether the totalisation-cum-withdrawal-benefit calculation applies at all turns entirely on whether the worker was, at some point, "covered under a social security agreement" — a status earned by working in a specific SSA-partner country, not a status that has to be true of the worker's current country of residence.

2. What that means for the India → SSA-country → US sequence

Take a concrete version of the fact pattern: an Indian professional works several years in India as a domestic EPF member, is then deputed to Germany (an SSA-partner country) and tagged as an International Worker for that assignment, accrues a further few years there under the India-Germany agreement, and leaves that employment — India's total eligible service, even after totalising the German years, still short of ten. Instead of returning to India or staying in Germany, the worker's next job is in the United States, which has no Totalization Agreement with India at all.

Read literally, para 14(1) resolves this in the worker's favor. The eligibility test is performed "on the date of exit" — the date the German (or India) EPF/EPS membership ended — using the totalisation benefit "as may be provided in the said [India-Germany] agreement." Nothing in the text reopens or re-tests that determination based on what the worker does afterward. The withdrawal benefit (or, if the aggregate service had cleared ten years, the monthly pension entitlement) is a status fixed at exit, not a status that must be maintained by continuing to live or work in an SSA-partner country. EPFO's own "International Workers – Social Security Agreements" page corroborates this reading directly, describing the point of these agreements as letting workers "get the benefit of totalisation period for deciding the eligibility for pension" and then "get the pension in the country where they choose to live" — language that contemplates the beneficiary settling anywhere, not only in India or the SSA-partner country that supplied the totalised service.

Why this is not just the general "NRI-in-a-non-SSA-country" rule

3. The binary is SSA-coverage-ever, not SSA-coverage-now

The clearest evidence that "covered under SSA" is a historical, not a current-residence, test comes from the flip side of the same rule. A detailed 2026 TaxGuru analysis of PF withdrawal for International Workers, read in full, confirms that a foreign national who is an International Worker without ever having been covered by an SSA cannot access PF before age 58 on any theory — "cessation of Indian employment does not, by itself, permit withdrawal for such [non-SSA] international workers." That article, like every other source found, addresses only the two-country case: was this worker, in relation to India, ever SSA-covered or not. None asks what happens when an SSA-covered worker's subsequent, unrelated employment is in a third country that itself has no agreement with India. Para 14(1)'s "date of exit" anchor is the answer, and no source found states it.

4. Falsification performed

Two competitors were read in full. The Bombay Chartered Accountants' Journal ("International Workers and Social Security Agreements: Growing Significance, an Overview") gives the most thorough non-official treatment found of India's SSA/IW regime, including a dedicated section on India-US negotiations: "For almost a decade, India and USA have had talks on the totalisation agreement, however, without much success," and a specific point that Indian workers on US assignments under ten years "forego their social security contributions when they return" because of the interaction between US ten-year vesting and Indian visa-length limits. It does not address a worker who has already banked SSA-country totalised service elsewhere and then relocates to the US — the three-country sequence this page is built on. TaxGuru's 2026 "Why Age 58 Still Matters (SSA vs Non-SSA Explained)" article, read in full, is precise on the binary SSA/non-SSA test but frames it only for a foreign national's inbound employment in India, never for an Indian worker's chained outbound history through more than one country. A dedicated search combining "date of exit," International Worker, EPS, totalisation and withdrawal benefit returned no content addressing the interaction directly. Neither Neeraj Bhagat & Co.'s 2021 IW explainer nor the Karnataka Employers' Association's Circular 31/2012 (which reproduces EPFO's own May 2012 clarification letter in full and was read directly rather than through paraphrase) goes past the two-country case either. EPFO's own FAQ page (pmvbry.epfindia.gov.in/faq-epfo) was checked directly but its answer content loads via an accordion this pass could not expand; the underlying scheme text at epfindia.gov.in was read instead and is the stronger primary source in any case.

5. What is confirmed and what is not

  • VERIFIED — para 43-A(9) and 43-A(14)(1) of the EPS Scheme, 1995, read verbatim at EPFO's own site, anchor the totalisation-and-withdrawal-benefit test to "the date of exit" and to SSA coverage that "may be" provided in the relevant agreement, not to the worker's country of residence at the time of the claim.
  • VERIFIED — EPFO's International Workers page states the beneficiary may "get the pension in the country where they choose to live," and separately states India currently has 20 partner countries under these agreements; the US is not among them, corroborated independently by a 2021 Moneycontrol report describing India-US totalisation talks as ongoing "despite years of negotiation" and by the BCAJ Journal's 2026 treatment repeating the same point.
  • INFERENCE, re-checked 2026-09-03 — that "date of exit" in para 14(1) means the date the worker's last SSA-country or India EPF/EPS-covered employment ends, and that a subsequent, wholly separate US job does not constitute a new "exit" that reopens the eligibility test. This is the most natural reading of the text and is consistent with EPFO's "pension where they choose to live" framing. Re-searched this session (EPFO's own FAQ page and general web search for "date of exit" totalisation guidance): still no EPFO circular, FAQ, or tribunal ruling addressing this specific three-country fact pattern was found; the only "date of exit" guidance located concerns UAN/claim-form mechanics (correcting the field before withdrawal), not the totalisation eligibility question this page raises. Status unchanged. A practitioner should confirm this reading with EPFO's International Workers Unit before relying on it for a specific claim. Re-checked again 2026-09-03 — searched EPFO's live circulars database directly (epfindia.gov.in/circulars, International Worker division, all 18 circulars indexed 2011–2024: SSA-implementation notices for Korea, Denmark, Luxembourg, France, Switzerland, Brazil and the Singapore/CECA arrangement, plus a 2022 final-withdrawal clarification) and located EPFO's own ~2010 "FAQs on International Workers" document (32 Q&As, reproduced by TaxGuru from the original EPFO release). Two questions come close but do not reach this page's fact pattern: Q22 addresses a third-country national eligible for benefit only if that third country has its own enabling SSA with both other countries — a nationality/domicile question, not the sequential-employment "date of exit" question this page asks. Q30 states IW status is retained "till the time he avails the benefits under a social security programme covered under that SSA" — relevant background, silent on whether an unrelated later job resets the exit date. None of the 18 indexed circulars addresses a three-country sequential-employment totalisation scenario. Confirmed absent across two independent searches now, not merely un-searched.
  • Not resolved — how a Certificate of Coverage and "excluded employee" status (which removes the IW tag entirely when a worker stays on India's own EPF system rather than the host country's, per EPFO's May 2012 circular) interacts with this sequence if the worker held a COC during the Germany leg rather than being IW-tagged there. That is a different, narrower branch of the same rule this page did not chase down.

Questions worth putting to EPFO or a preparer

  1. If totalised service (India plus one SSA-partner country) still falls short of ten years at the date of exit, is the Table 'D' withdrawal benefit calculated and payable regardless of where the worker lives or works afterward — and is there any EPFO circular or FAQ that states this for a subsequent move to a non-SSA country specifically?
  2. Does holding a Certificate of Coverage during the SSA-country assignment (rather than being IW-tagged there) change which paragraph of para 43-A applies, and does it change the answer to question 1?
  3. Is there a time limit on filing the withdrawal-benefit claim after the date of exit, and does filing from a non-SSA country like the US create any additional documentation requirement beyond the standard NRI EPF withdrawal process?
