All 19 unsettled questions

Every page in this series in one place, grouped by jurisdiction. Each one reads a primary source — a statute, a treaty article, a revenue procedure, a manual — directly, rather than repeating the secondary commentary.

These are not predictions or recommendations. Each page identifies a specific point where popular guidance on US, UK or Canadian tax treatment of Indian assets (PPF, EPF, NPS, HUF property, and related cross-border questions) rests on an assumption the primary source does not actually support — and says so, with the citation.

United States — 13 pages

  • Part 1: PPF, EPF, NPS and ULIPs — four questions US guidance answers too confidently

    PPF, EPF, NPS and ULIPs read against IRS revenue procedures and treaty text directly — four points where common US tax guidance overstates its footing.

  • Part 2: joint accounts and FBAR

    Being named on a parent's Indian bank account can trigger FBAR reporting regardless of whose money it is, read against 31 CFR 1010.350 directly.

  • Part 3: selling inherited property

    FEMA's repatriation route for inherited Indian property differs from the route for self-purchased property, and the governing TDS section was renumbered in 2026.

  • Part 4: the green-card treaty election

    Filing as a nonresident under the India-US treaty tie-breaker can be used against a green-card holder at the border, per the regulation's own text.

  • Part 5: LRS TCS and the US foreign tax credit

    India's 20% LRS tax collected at source is creditable against Indian tax, but whether it survives as a US foreign tax credit is a separate, rarely-asked question.

  • Part 6: HUF classification and the karta’s liability

    No IRS ruling has ever classified a Hindu Undivided Family; the karta's unlimited liability may decide whether it's a trust or business entity under Treasury regs.

  • Part 7: the bank’s indicia beats your FATCA form

    Under the India-US FATCA IGA, a bank's own account indicia can override a customer's FATCA self-certification form — most update guidance ignores this.

  • Part 8: EPF and gratuity lump sums vs the treaty pension definition

    The US-India treaty's own Technical Explanation indicates an EPF or gratuity lump sum doesn't qualify as an Article 20 “pension” — most guidance assumes otherwise.

  • Part 9: India’s fiscal year and the US foreign tax credit

    India's April-March tax year doesn't map cleanly onto a single US tax year for Form 1116 foreign tax credit timing — the governing regulation is rarely named.

  • Part 10: the foreign death tax credit that can’t reach India

    26 U.S.C. §2014's foreign death tax credit requires a foreign estate tax “actually paid” — India repealed its estate tax in 1985, so the credit may not reach it.

  • Part 11: the fractional interest discount meets an HUF interest

    US fractional-interest valuation discounts were built for fixed shares in a partition court; a Hindu Undivided Family coparcenary interest is structurally different.

  • Part 12: EPF totalisation across a third, non-SSA country

    India totalised pension service with Germany under a Totalization Agreement; the US has none. What EPFO's own text says about that gap is rarely discussed.

  • Part 13: the para 83/43A litigation and EPF Scheme 2026

    Two Indian High Courts split on whether a US employee's EPF contributions are capped at ₹15,000 wages; the EPF Scheme 2026 quietly picked a side.

United Kingdom — 5 pages

  • Part 14 (UK): the 2017 transitional-protection folklore

    The widely-cited “2017 transitional protection” for Indian EPF under UK pension rules doesn't match what HMRC's own manuals and the statute actually say.

  • Part 15 (UK): is EPF an “overseas pension scheme” at all

    ITEPA s.574A's Step 3 deduction on a UK lump sum applies only if Indian EPF itself qualifies as an “overseas pension scheme” — a question most guides skip.

  • Part 18 (UK): the FIG regime vs the DTAA tax-sparing credit

    The UK's 4-year FIG regime has a hidden cost for NRE interest income when a DTAA tax-sparing credit under Article 24 sits unused right next to it.

  • Part 19 (UK): does any Indian scheme pass the QROPS transfer test

    HMRC's live ROPS list names 35 Indian schemes — all commercial annuity products, none named NPS or EPF — read against Regulation 3 and the 25% transfer charge.

  • Part 20 (UK): the Temporary Repatriation Facility vs your India tax credit

    HMRC's own manual: a TRF designation is net of Indian tax already paid, and “no further credit can be claimed” — a binary trade-off no competitor states.

Canada — 2 pages

  • Part 16 (Canada): T1135 reporting and the Article 18 treaty question

    Whether Indian EPF must be reported on Form T1135, and how Article 18 of the Canada-India tax treaty actually treats it — read from the statute and treaty text.

  • Part 17 (Canada): departure tax and the excluded-right-or-interest test

    Does Canada's departure-tax deemed-disposition rule reach an Indian EPF or PPF balance under the “excluded right or interest” exclusion? Read against s.128.1 directly.
